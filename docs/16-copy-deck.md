# 16 — Copy deck & voice

## Voice

Five rules, in priority order. When they conflict, the earlier one wins.

1. **Specific over impressive.** "Nineteen dairy plants between 5,000 and 200,000
   LPD" beats "extensive dairy experience". Every claim carries a number or it
   gets cut.
2. **Say the uncomfortable thing.** "About one engagement in six concludes the
   client should not expand." This is the voice's whole differentiator — a
   competitor can copy a layout, not a willingness to lose a sale.
3. **The reader's words, not ours.** Service pages open with symptoms in the
   promoter's language: "Your file has been 'under process' at the branch for five
   months." Recognition converts; description does not.
4. **Plain, not casual.** No jargon, no exclamation marks, no "let's", no emoji.
   A 55-year-old promoter and a credit officer are both in the audience.
5. **Indian English, Indian numbering.** ₹, lakh, crore, `12,34,567`, "tonne",
   "LPD", "mithai". No conversion to dollars or millions.

### Banned

"India's leading", "world-class", "end-to-end solutions", "synergy", "leverage"
as a verb, "cutting-edge", "one-stop shop", "passionate about", "unlock",
"empower", "revolutionise", "seamless", any superlative without a figure.

### Load-bearing sentences

These carry the positioning. Change them only deliberately:

> We take no commissions from machinery suppliers or lenders, which is why we can
> tell you when a project is too big.

> A partner reads every enquiry. You will get either a calendar link within one
> business day, or a straight answer that we are not the right firm for this.

> Most lost subsidy claims are not lost on eligibility. They are lost on
> sequence — an order placed three weeks too early.

> Saying this publicly costs us enquiries. It also means that when we say yes,
> you can believe it.

## Page templates

### Service page
* **Eyebrow** `{Practice} practice`
* **H1** The outcome, not the service. "Bankable project reports that survive the
  credit committee."
* **"You are probably here because"** Five numbered symptoms, second person,
  present tense.
* **Approach** 4–5 steps. At least one must name a discipline that costs us
  something: "We have stopped 14 projects at this stage. That is a feature."
* **Deliverables** Each with the week it lands.
* **Results** Medians with their basis. Never a best case.
* **Fees** Published ranges with "best for" framing.
* **FAQs** Must include the sceptical question — "Can you guarantee the loan will
  be sanctioned?" — answered honestly ("No, and anyone who guarantees it is
  either lying or planning something you do not want to be part of").
* **Closing CTA** Names the practice and the partner.

### Industry page
* **H1** The sector's central tension. "From chilling centre to value-added plant,
  without stranding capital."
* **Market context** One paragraph, opportunity *and* trap.
* **Challenges** "The five things that break dairy projects" — specific failures
  we have fixed.
* **Benchmarks** Four figures, with the price year and a caveat that they are for
  sanity-checking a quotation, not budgeting a project.
* **Schemes** Table with administering body and indicative benefit.

### Case study
* **H1** The result as a sentence. "A ₹18 crore dairy expansion funded, built and
  running at 86% OEE."
* **Challenge** One sentence naming the number that was wrong.
* **Context** Three paragraphs, including what had already failed.
* **Approach** Phase by phase, with the phase the client least enjoyed.
* **Results** Every figure with its baseline.
* **Quote** Real, and preferably slightly barbed. "Being told to halve the project
  was not what we paid for."

### Article
* **Title** The reader's question, or the specific claim.
* **Key takeaways** 3–5 — the answer in 20 seconds.
* **Opening** The failure pattern, not a definition. Never "In today's competitive
  landscape".
* **Body** Numbers, tables, sequences. One pull-quote maximum.
* **Closing note** The standing disclaimer with the price year.

## Forms

* **Submit buttons** Name the outcome: "Request a discovery call", "Email me the
  file".
* **Under the submit button** "Average response time last quarter: 5 hours 40
  minutes, in business hours."
* **Consent** Says what we will not do: "No data is sold or shared with lenders
  without my written consent."
* **Errors** Instructive, not scolding: "City helps us route you to the right
  desk."
* **Success** "A partner reviews every enquiry personally. You'll hear from us
  within one business day with either a calendar link or a straight answer that
  we're not the right firm for this."

## Careers

**H1** A small firm where you own the work end to end

**Lede** We do not run a pyramid. There is no layer of juniors preparing slides
for a partner who has not read them. If you join, you will be on site, in the
credit committee meeting, and answerable for the recommendation.

**Deliberate deterrent, on every role page:** "This role involves travel and
factory floors. If spending a day a week in a processing plant sounds like the
wrong job, it probably is — and we would rather you knew that now than in month
three."

**Application ask:** one paragraph on a business problem the candidate has
actually solved — what the number was before, what they did, what it was
afterwards.

## Localisation notes

When Marathi and Gujarati versions are produced, translate the *argument*, not the
sentences. The load-bearing sentences above rely on English understatement that
does not survive literal translation; brief the translator on the intent
("we refuse supplier commissions, and this is why you can trust our advice")
rather than handing over the string.
